📚 API & JASO Knowledge Base
Understand the letters on your oil label — a short guide to service categories.

🅰️ API "S" Series — Petrol Engines

"S" = Service (petrol). Progression: SJ < SL < SM < SN < SN Plus < SP

  • SPLatest — LSPI protection, timing chain wear, turbo/GDI. Backward compatible.
  • SN PlusInterim — addresses LSPI in TGDI engines. API SP includes all SN Plus.
  • SN2010 — better high‑temp deposits & fuel economy.
  • SM2004 — improved oxidation & wear protection.
  • SL2001 — better high‑temperature deposit control.
  • SJ1996 — improved deposit control & oil consumption.

🛢️ API "C" Series — Diesel Engines

"C" = Commercial (diesel). Progression: CF‑4 < CG‑4 < CH‑4 < CI‑4 < CI‑4 Plus < CJ‑4 < CK‑4

  • CK‑42017 — modern diesel, DPF/SCR compatible. Exceeds CI‑4 Plus.
  • CJ‑42006 — low‑ash, DPF compatible, 2007 emissions.
  • CI‑4 PlusHigher protection vs. soot‑viscosity & shear loss.
  • CI‑42002 — EGR engines, superior soot dispersal.
  • CH‑41998 — up to 0.5% sulphur fuel. (removed from catalogue)
  • CG‑41995 — severe duty. (removed from catalogue)
  • CF‑41990 — basic soot control. (removed from catalogue)
⚠️ Note: CH‑4, CG‑4 and CF‑4 entries were removed from the catalogue because they were listed inaccurately. Only verified categories remain.

🏍️ JASO — Motorcycle Oils

JASO T 903 — wet clutch friction classification.

  • MA2High friction — wet clutch, maximum grip. Most modern bikes.
  • MA1Medium friction — wet clutch, balance of grip & smooth engagement.
  • MAOlder standard, now split into MA1 & MA2.
  • MBLow friction — dry clutch only. Not for wet clutch bikes.
⚠️ Important: For any motorcycle with a wet clutch, always use a JASO MA rated oil. Standard car oils may cause clutch slippage.

🇺🇸🇯🇵 ILSAC & ❄️ W‑Grade

ILSAC = American & Japanese automaker standard.

  • GF‑6ABased on API SP — adds fuel economy & emission system protection.
  • GF‑6BFor 0W‑16 grades only (very low viscosity).
  • GF‑5Based on API SN — previous generation.

W‑Grade explained (e.g. 5W‑30):

  • Before WCold‑weather flow. Lower = better cold start. 0W best, 20W weakest.
  • After WViscosity at 100°C. Higher = thicker when hot. 20 thin, 50 thick.
